# Naver

**Category**: Economy
**Key figures**: Lee Hae-jin (founder), Choi Soo-yeon (CEO from 2023), Han Seong-sook (CEO 2017–2022)

## Summary

Naver Corporation (NAVER Corp.) is South Korea's largest internet company and search portal, a dominant platform operator controlling the country's internet search market and operating an integrated ecosystem spanning web-based email, news aggregation, social communities, [webtoon](../culture-society/webtoons.md) distribution, digital payments, cloud computing, and e-commerce. Founded on June 2, 1999, by Lee Hae-jin and four co-founders, Naver grew from a search-engine startup into a diversified tech conglomerate listed on the KOSPI stock exchange under the ticker NAVER. As of mid-2024, the company's market capitalization exceeded 20 trillion Korean won (approximately USD 15 billion), making it one of South Korea's most valuable publicly listed companies.

Naver's rise parallels the explosion of Korean internet adoption in the 1990s–2000s, during which the company captured approximately 70–80% of the Korean search market—a dominance maintained for over two decades despite competition from Google. Beyond search, Naver operates Naver News (the country's largest news aggregation platform), Naver Café (Korea's largest online community platform), Naver Webtoon (a leading digital comics platform with global operations), Naver Pay (digital payment system), Naver Cloud Platform, and the LINE messaging service (dominant in Japan, Taiwan, Thailand, and Indonesia). The company's integrated ecosystem—where search, content, community, commerce, and services are tightly interwoven—defines Korean internet culture in ways few Western platforms parallel.

Naver represents both the peculiarities of Korean internet capitalism (where portal dominance and vertical integration create powerful network effects resistant to outside competition) and South Korea's capacity for globally competitive [technology-sector](../economy/technology-sector.md) innovation.

## History and Founding

The South Korean internet began developing rapidly in the mid-1990s, supported by government investment in broadband infrastructure under the Kim Young-sam and Kim Dae-jung administrations. The initial search market was dominated by Daum Communications, which launched Daum.net in 1995. Naver began in 1997 as an in-house venture team at Samsung SDS led by Lee Hae-jin, and on June 2, 1999, it was spun off as an independent company, Naver Comm., launching Naver.com ("Navigator") as a web directory and search service.

Naver's early breakthrough came from superior search-result ranking algorithms and a distinctive "comprehensive search" (통합검색, tonghap-geomsaek) interface that displayed results from multiple categories—web pages, encyclopedias, Q&A communities, images, and news—in a single organized page rather than a pure ten-blue-links format. This approach suited Korean internet users who expected the portal to be a one-stop destination.

By 2002, Naver had captured the majority of Korea's search market. The company merged with gaming company Hangame in 2002 to form NHN Corporation, becoming a major force in both search and online gaming. NHN went public on the KOSPI in 2002, raising capital for platform expansion.

In 2013, NHN split into two companies: **NHN Corporation** (retaining the gaming and entertainment businesses) and **Naver Corporation** (retaining search, portal, and platform businesses). This restructuring reflected the diverging strategic focuses of the two business lines and allowed Naver to pursue its technology and platform ambitions independently.

## Growth and Diversification (2000s–2010s)

In the 2000s, Naver expanded aggressively beyond search. **Naver Knowledge iN** (지식iN, Knowledge IN), launched in 2002, became Korea's dominant Q&A platform—a precursor to services like Yahoo Answers or Quora—generating user-created content that Naver indexed in its search results, creating a powerful content flywheel. By the late 2000s, Naver's Knowledge iN contained hundreds of millions of questions and answers, making it the de facto source for many Korean-language informational queries.

**Naver Café** (커뮤니티 카페), a Korean-style online community platform where interest groups create and manage their own community spaces, became integral to Korean internet culture, with millions of active communities around hobbies, entertainment, consumer products, sports fans, and social causes. Unlike Western social networks organized around individual profiles, Naver Café organized communities around topics and groups.

**Naver News** aggregates content from hundreds of publishers, making Naver a gatekeeper for news consumption in Korea. This role has been periodically controversial: critics argue that Naver's algorithmic prioritization of certain articles and publishers significantly shapes public discourse and gives the company outsized editorial influence over Korean society. The government and press advocacy groups have repeatedly raised concerns about Naver's role in news distribution.

## LINE: Naver's Global Messenger

Naver's most significant international product is **LINE**, a mobile messaging application launched in June 2011 in response to the disruption of Japanese communications networks during the March 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami. LINE was developed by NHN Japan (later renamed Line Corporation) specifically for the Japanese market, offering free messaging when cellular networks were strained.

LINE grew explosively in Japan: by 2012 it had 50 million users; by 2014, 400 million. It became the dominant messaging app in Japan, Taiwan, Thailand, and Indonesia, markets where it achieved penetration rates exceeding 50% of smartphone users. LINE expanded into sticker commerce (proprietary character-based digital stickers became a significant revenue source), LINE Pay, LINE News, LINE Manga (a Japanese digital comics platform), LINE TV, and financial services (LINE Bank).

In 2021, Line Corporation merged with SoftBank's Yahoo Japan to form **Z Holdings Corporation** (later renamed LY Corporation in 2023), creating one of Japan's largest internet companies. Naver holds its stake through a 50/50 joint venture with SoftBank (A Holdings), which controls LY Corporation, making the Japanese operations a major component of Naver's consolidated financials. The LINE-Yahoo Japan merger created regulatory and data-governance complications that drew scrutiny from Japanese authorities, who in 2024 questioned Naver's handling of user data security following a LINE data breach.

## The Webtoon Platform and Content Strategy

Naver's [webtoon](../culture-society/webtoons.md) strategy has been among its most consequential content investments. Naver launched its webtoon service in 2004, before the format became globally recognized, making it among the earliest digital comics platforms optimized for vertical-scroll mobile viewing. By the early 2010s, Naver Webtoon had tens of thousands of series, with top creators earning annual revenues in the billions of won.

Naver Webtoon expanded internationally through the "Webtoon" brand in English-language markets (2014), becoming a dominant global platform by the late 2010s. In 2021, Naver acquired **Wattpad**, a Canadian storytelling platform with tens of millions of users and hundreds of millions of stories predominantly by women writers, for approximately USD 600 million, creating a combined content platform called "Wattpad Webtoon Studios." The Wattpad acquisition extended Naver's reach into prose fiction, fan fiction, and a predominantly English-speaking user base complementing webtoon's visual-comics focus.

In 2024, Webtoon Entertainment went public on NASDAQ (ticker WBTN) in an IPO that valued the company at approximately USD 2.7 billion, representing Naver's successful international extension of a Korean-origin content format.

## Market Position and Business Model

Naver's primary revenue streams include:

1. **Search and portal advertising**: ~70–80% share of the Korean search market generates substantial cost-per-click and display advertising revenue. Naver's search advertising system uses a bidding model that distributes clicks across multiple "power links" at the top of search results.

2. **E-commerce and marketplace services**: Naver Shopping (스마트스토어, SmartStore) aggregates millions of merchants; Naver earns transaction fees and advertising from the platform. As of 2023, Naver's e-commerce gross merchandise volume (GMV) exceeded 30 trillion Korean won annually.

3. **Content and digital services**: Webtoon subscriptions, series coin-purchase systems, digital music (Naver Now), and premium content access.

4. **Naver Pay and financial services**: A digital wallet and payment platform integrated across Naver's ecosystem. Naver Pay competes directly with Kakao Pay and Samsung Pay.

5. **Naver Cloud Platform**: Enterprise and public-sector cloud services competing with AWS, Microsoft Azure, and NHN Cloud in the Korean market.

6. **LINE Japan financials**: Consolidated revenues from LY Corporation's Japanese operations.

## Competition with Kakao

Throughout the 2010s, Naver's primary domestic rival became **Kakao Corporation**, whose KakaoTalk messaging app (launched March 2010) captured approximately 93% of Korean smartphone messaging. Starting from messaging, Kakao expanded into games (Kakao Games), content (Kakao Page, Kakao Webtoon), payments (KakaoPay), banking (KakaoBank, launched July 2017, among South Korea's first internet-only banks), mobility (Kakao Mobility, operating Korea's dominant taxi-hailing app), and numerous other verticals.

The **Naver–Kakao duopoly** defines Korean internet business, with the two companies competing across search, content, payments, cloud, and AI. Korean antitrust authorities (the Korea Fair Trade Commission, KFTC) have periodically investigated both companies for market dominance and anti-competitive practices—including Naver for alleged preferential treatment of its own services in search results—though neither has faced structural breakup proposals comparable to Western regulatory actions against Google or Meta.

## AI and HyperCLOVA X

Responding to the disruption of generative AI, Naver has invested significantly in its own large language model capabilities. In 2021, Naver released **HyperCLOVA**, a Korean-language large language model with 204 billion parameters—at release, among the largest LLMs in existence, larger than OpenAI's GPT-3, and the largest Korean-language model by a significant margin. HyperCLOVA was trained primarily on Korean-language data, giving it capabilities in Korean that general multilingual models could not match.

In 2023, Naver released **HyperCLOVA X**, an updated and expanded model incorporating multimodal capabilities and deeper integration with Naver's search and platform services. Naver has positioned HyperCLOVA X as the foundation for AI-powered search (replacing keyword-based search with conversational AI responses), enterprise AI services, and productivity tools.

Naver's AI strategy distinguishes it from global competitors by focusing on deep Korean-language capability: while OpenAI's ChatGPT and Google's Gemini offer Korean-language support, Naver argues that its Korean-first training gives HyperCLOVA X superior performance for Korean cultural, linguistic, and contextual nuances. This positions Naver's AI as a sovereign alternative to American AI services, aligning with Korean government interest in domestic AI capability.

## Cultural Significance

Beyond its economic role, Naver is culturally significant in Korea as the primary gateway to the internet for most Korean users. For many Koreans, particularly those who came of age in the 2000s, the distinction between "the internet" and "Naver" was blurred: the company provided news, search, community, email, maps, encyclopedias, and entertainment through a single portal. This cultural dominance has given Naver outsized influence over Korean digital discourse, content distribution, and news consumption.

Naver's encyclopedia service (지식백과, Jisikbaekgwa) has become the de facto reference source for Korean-language information across numerous domains, partnering with academic publishers, medical institutions, and cultural organizations. The platform's role in Korean knowledge infrastructure parallels that of Wikipedia in English-language contexts, though with Naver's commercial mediation.

The company's integration of AI assistant features into search, its role in the careers of thousands of webtoon creators, and its management of Japan's largest internet ecosystem make it one of the most consequential companies in East Asian digital culture.
