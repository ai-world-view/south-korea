# North Gyeongsang Province

**Category**: Geography  
**Key figures**: N/A

## Summary

North Gyeongsang Province (경상북도, Gyeongbuk), located in southeastern South Korea, is the country's largest province by area (approximately 19,027 km²) and a major industrial and cultural region. It is closely associated with the adjacent **Daegu Metropolitan City** (대구광역시), which was administratively separated from the province in 1981 to become a directly governed metropolitan city; together the two jurisdictions form the cultural and political "TK" (대경, Daegu-Gyeongbuk) region. The province proper has a population of approximately 2.6 million (excluding Daegu), anchored by the industrial port city of Pohang and the heritage city of Gyeongju. North Gyeongsang encompasses diverse landscapes ranging from coastal areas along the East Sea to inland highlands in the Taebaek range. The province's economy historically centered on agriculture and fishing but transformed through the 20th century into a hub for steel manufacturing (POSCO), petrochemicals, electronics, and automotive production. North Gyeongsang Province is home to Gyeongju, Korea's premier heritage-tourism destination and seat of the ancient Silla kingdom's capital for over a millennium.

The province's cultural identity reflects layers of Korean history: preserved Joseon-era aristocratic villages (hanok architecture, yangban heritage), Buddhist temples from the Three Kingdoms and Silla periods, and postwar industrial modernization. Daegu, as a major metropolitan center, drives much of the province's economic and cultural activity, though Pohang's steel and petrochemical complexes, Andong's Confucian heritage sites, and Gyeongju's archaeological significance distribute importance across the region.

## Geography and Climate

North Gyeongsang Province stretches across southeastern Korea, bounded by the East Sea (Sea of Japan) to the east. The Taebaek Mountains, Korea's eastern backbone range, run through the province's northern and western regions; elevations in the higher peaks exceed 1,500 meters. The province includes the volcanic Ulleung Island and the smaller Dokdo/Takeshima islets in its maritime territory (the latter a territory disputed with Japan).

The coastal regions feature fishing towns and port cities; the interior comprises agricultural flatlands, river valleys, and forested highlands. North Gyeongsang experiences a humid subtropical to temperate continental climate, with coastal areas receiving maritime influences. Annual precipitation is moderate in the west and higher along the coast. Winters are cold, particularly inland; summers are warm and often humid.

## Major Cities and Regions

**Daegu** (대구) — Administratively a separate Metropolitan City (not part of North Gyeongsang Province since 1981), Daegu (population ~2.35 million as of 2023) functions as the TK region's economic and cultural hub and is historically and politically inseparable from the province. Known as the "Manchester of Korea" for its 20th-century textile and garment industry, Daegu has diversified into electronics, automotive components, and medical devices. The Suseong district is its primary upscale residential and business area; Dongdaegu Station is a major rail hub on the KTX bullet-train network. The **2003 Daegu subway fire** (February 18, 2003), in which an arsonist set fire to a subway car at Jungangno Station, killed 192 people and injured 148 others; it remains one of the world's deadliest rapid-transit disasters and prompted sweeping revisions to fire safety standards for underground rail systems across South Korea. The provincial government's administrative capital was relocated from Daegu to **Andong** in 2016 as part of a policy to decentralize governance within the province.

**Pohang** (포항, population ~490,000) — Korea's steel capital and a major industrial port on Yeongil Bay. Pohang is dominated by **POSCO** (Pohang Iron and Steel Company, 포스코), established in 1968 with US$119 million in Japanese reparations funds under the government of Park Chung-hee, with the first blast furnace firing in 1973. POSCO became the world's largest steelmaker by production volume in the 1990s, and by the 2010s consistently ranked among the world's top three producers (alongside ArcelorMittal and Nippon Steel). The Pohang Steel Works is an integrated mill covering approximately 8.54 km² on reclaimed land and capable of producing over 18 million tonnes of crude steel per year. The 2022 **Pohang earthquake** (September 5.4 magnitude) damaged POSCO's facilities and temporarily halted production, causing global steel supply ripple effects. Port facilities at Pohang handle iron ore (imported from Australia and Brazil), coal, and finished steel exports; annual cargo throughput exceeds 100 million tonnes. **POSTECH** (Pohang University of Science and Technology, founded 1986), funded by POSCO, consistently ranks among South Korea's top engineering universities and is a major center for materials science, laser physics, and biomedical engineering research.

**Gyeongju** (경주, population ~248,000) — The former capital of the Silla kingdom for over a millennium (57 BCE–935 CE), Gyeongju is sometimes called a "museum without walls" due to its exceptional concentration of archaeological sites, temples, and burial mounds. The city's historic areas were inscribed on the **UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000** under the designation "Gyeongju Historic Areas," encompassing Namsan hill (covered in stone Buddhas and temples), the Royal Tumuli Park (vast burial mounds of the Silla royal family, some exceeding 22 metres in height), Wolseong fortress site, Anapji pond (a reconstructed royal garden), and Hwangnyongsa temple site. **Bulguksa Temple** (불국사, founded traditionally 528 CE, rebuilt at its current scale c. 774 CE) and the adjacent **Seokguram Grotto** (석굴암), an artificial stone-domed cave housing a masterwork granite Buddha and carved relief panels, were inscribed separately as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1995. **Cheomseongdae** (첨성대, c. 632–647 CE), a bottle-shaped stone observatory, is East Asia's oldest surviving astronomical observation structure. The Gyeongju National Museum houses the Silla gold crowns (delicate openwork gold headpieces inlaid with jade comma-shaped pendants), the **Emille Bell** (성덕대왕신종, 771 CE, the largest surviving Korean bronze bell at 18.9 tonnes), and thousands of artifacts from Silla royal tombs.

**Andong** (안동) — A regional cultural center in the interior, Andong is celebrated for its preservation of Joseon-era yangban (aristocratic) culture, with numerous hanok villages and Confucian academies (seowon). The Andong Mask Dance (Andong-mask) and traditional crafts are major draws. The city serves as a gateway to inland Gyeongbuk heritage and traditional food culture.

**Ulleung Island** (울릉도) — A small volcanic island 130 kilometers east of the mainland, Ulleung Island is administered by North Gyeongsang Province. It is a tourism and fishing hub, with steep cliffs, pristine forests, and limited road infrastructure. The island is home to the endemic Ulleung bellflower and has a population of approximately 10,000 residents.

## Economy

North Gyeongsang's economy rests on four pillars: **steel and heavy industry** (POSCO, in Pohang), **electronics manufacturing** (the Gumi industrial complex), **automotive and machinery components**, and **agriculture and fishing**. (The Ulsan shipyards and the Onsan petrochemical complex, sometimes grouped with the region's industry, lie in the separate Ulsan Metropolitan City to the south.)

**Steel and Manufacturing**: POSCO, headquartered in Pohang, is a global steel giant. The Pohang works produce both commodity steel and specialty products (electrical steel, stainless, coatings). Ancillary industries (rolling mills, fabrication, machinery) cluster around the main facility.

**Electronics**: The Gumi National Industrial Complex (구미국가산업단지), developed from 1969 under the government of Gumi-born Park Chung-hee, is one of South Korea's largest electronics and mobile-device manufacturing hubs, long anchored by major Samsung Electronics and LG (Display and Electronics) plants producing displays, mobile phones, and optical and communications equipment.

**Automotive and Machinery**: Component suppliers across the province — and in the adjacent Daegu Metropolitan City — supply transmissions, electronics, and modules to Hyundai and Kia, alongside general machinery and textile machinery makers.

**Agriculture and Fishing**: Inland regions produce rice, barley, apples (Andong and Cheongsong are noted apple-growing areas), and vegetables; coastal areas support fishing fleets targeting mackerel, squid, and snow crab (a Yeongdeok specialty). Rural depopulation has pressured this sector since the 1980s.

**Electronics and R&D**: POSTECH and other research institutions, along with the Gumi electronics cluster, anchor a growing technology sector.

The province's economy faced structural challenges in the 2000s and 2010s as textile manufacturing declined and global steel prices fell. However, postwar recovery and diversification into higher-value-added sectors (specialty chemicals, advanced materials, semiconductors) have stabilized growth.

## History

North Gyeongsang Province's territory corresponds roughly to the ancient kingdom of Silla (57 BCE–668 CE, though fragmented until 668), which unified the Korean peninsula in 668 CE under Kim Yu-shin and subsequently dominated the Korean peninsula for 300 years as Unified Silla (668–935 CE). Gyeongju served as Silla's capital, becoming a major Buddhist and cultural center. The Silla nobility (aristocrats) developed the bone-rank system and a sophisticated administrative structure.

After Silla's decline, the region became part of Goryeo (918–1392) and subsequently the Joseon dynasty (1392–1910). During the Joseon period, the region became a heartland of Confucian scholarship and yangban culture, with numerous seowon (Confucian academies) and preserved villages reflecting aristocratic traditions.

The Japanese colonial period (1910–1945) saw industrial development focused on ports (Pohang) and resource extraction, though the region remained primarily agricultural. After liberation (1945), the Korean War divided families and disrupted development. Daegu was a crucial hub during the Busan Perimeter phase of the war (1950–1951).

Postwar reconstruction and the Park Chung-hee era (1961–1979) brought rapid industrialization. The establishment of POSCO in 1973 transformed Pohang from a fishing town into a steel metropolis. The 1970s and 1980s saw auto-component and electronics manufacturing grow around Daegu. The province became a center of South Korea's developmental-state growth model.

## Culture and Society

North Gyeongsang Province's cultural identity reflects its Silla heritage and yangban aristocratic traditions.

**Silla Buddhism and Architecture**: Temples such as Bulguksa and Seokguram, built during Unified Silla's golden age (8th century), exemplify Korean Buddhist art and remain major pilgrimage and tourism sites. The province's many smaller temples and pagodas also reflect this heritage.

**Yangban Heritage**: Preserved villages (particularly Andong's Hahoe and Yangdong villages) showcase traditional hanok architecture, Confucian rituals, and aristocratic lifestyle. The seowon academy system was a major intellectual center for Neo-Confucian scholarship during the Joseon dynasty.

**Traditional Arts**: Andong masks and the Andong mask dance (Hahoe-masks) are UNESCO intangible cultural heritage. Traditional music, calligraphy, and handicrafts remain practiced by cultural organizations and individual artisans.

**Festivals**: Gyeongju's cultural tourism draws millions annually to the Korean Wave Festival (K-pop and drama tourism), the Silla Cultural Festival, and the annual Asian Film Festival of Daegu. Daegu hosts the biennial Daegu Photo Biennale and other contemporary art events.

**Education**: POSTECH (Pohang University of Science and Technology) and Andong National University are major research institutions. The region contributes significantly to Korea's STEM education and industrial R&D.

**Sports**: Daegu hosted the 2003 Summer Universiade and the 2011 World Championships in Athletics. Pohang Steelers (one of the most successful clubs in K League football) and Daegu FC are major professional teams.

## Demographics and Challenges

North Gyeongsang Province proper has approximately 2.6 million residents (2023), with the adjacent Daegu Metropolitan City adding another ~2.35 million to the broader regional population. The province's population (excluding Daegu) has been in steady decline since peaking around 1985; the rural interior has lost population particularly sharply, with many counties (군, gun) recording more deaths than births annually. Aging demographics are severe in inland agricultural regions — some rural counties have median ages exceeding 55 years, among the highest in South Korea.

**Unemployment and Economic Transition**: The decline of textile manufacturing and global steel oversupply have pressured employment. Young people migrate to Seoul and other major cities for better job prospects, deepening rural depopulation.

**Environmental Issues**: Heavy industry around Pohang and Onsan has created air and water-quality concerns. The province has implemented emissions controls and environmental remediation programs, though industrial pollution remains a public-health issue.

**Air Quality**: As part of the Yeongdong region, North Gyeongsang experiences seasonal yellow dust (hwangsa) from Central Asia and China, compounded by local industrial emissions.

## Politics and Regional Identity

North Gyeongsang Province, particularly Daegu, is a conservative stronghold forming the "TK" (대경, Taegu-Kyungbuk / Daegu-Gyeongbuk) political bloc. Park Chung-hee (1917–1979), who governed South Korea from 1961 to 1979, was born in Gumi, North Gyeongsang Province, and his developmental-state policies — including the POSCO steel complex, the Gyeongbu Expressway (connecting Seoul to Busan via Daegu), and the Heavy and Chemical Industry drive of the 1970s — disproportionately benefited the region. This political-economic linkage created a durable conservative affiliation: the TK region delivered overwhelming margins for conservative presidential candidates in every election from 1963 to the 2020s.

The region's conservatism is often contrasted with the progressive Honam (호남, Gwangju and North/South Jeolla provinces) bloc in Korea's most persistent regional cleavage. Park Geun-hye (1952–), Park Chung-hee's daughter and South Korea's first female president (2013–2017, impeached), was born in Daegu and drew her strongest support from the TK region before her removal from office. The regional rivalry between TK and Honam has moderated since the 2010s as younger voters weight policy issues over regional identity.
