# South Gyeongsang Province (Gyeongnam)

**South Gyeongsang Province** (경상남도, *Gyeongsang Namdo* or *Gyeongnam*) is the
south-eastern province of South Korea, bordered by North Gyeongsang Province to
the north, the Jeolla provinces to the west, the Busan and Ulsan metropolitan areas
to the east, and the Korea Strait (South Sea) to the south. The
province's administrative territory covers approximately 10,534 km² with a resident
population of roughly 3.3 million (2024 estimate), excluding the adjacent metropolitan
cities of Busan, Ulsan, and Daegu, which were historically part of the Gyeongnam
administrative region but are now independently governed. Its capital is **Changwon**.

## Geography and Climate

The landscape is defined by the convergence of two major mountain systems: the **Sobaek
Range** (to the west, peaking around 1,000–1,400 m in Jirisan National Park, the
highest inland mountain in South Korea at 1,915 m) and the eastern extension of the
**Taebaek Range**. These highlands descend to a coastal plain and river delta in the
south and south-west.

The **Nakdong River** (낙동강), Korea's longest river at 521 km, rises in North
Gyeongsang Province and flows south through the province, emptying into the Korea Strait
at the Nakdong Estuary near Busan. The river basin, approximately 23,860 km², provides
irrigation for the province's agricultural zones and feeds a series of dams and
reservoirs (including the Hapcheon Dam and the Imha Dam on tributaries) that supply
hydroelectric power and drinking water for the greater Busan–Changwon metropolitan area.

The climate is temperate maritime: warm, humid summers (mean July temperature in
Changwon ~27°C), mild winters (mean January ~2°C), and annual rainfall of 1,200–1,500 mm,
concentrated in the June–September monsoon season. The south-facing coast is vulnerable
to typhoons tracking north through the Korea Strait in late summer and autumn; Typhoon
Rusa (2002) caused catastrophic flooding in the province, killing more than 200 people
and destroying hundreds of farms and structures.

### Islands

The province encompasses major inhabited islands on its south coast:

- **Geoje Island** (Geoje-do): South Korea's second-largest island (383 km²), connected to
  Tongyeong and the mainland by bridge since 1971 and directly to Busan since December 2010
  via the Geoga Fixed Link (a combination of cable-stayed bridges and a 3.7 km immersed-tube
  undersea tunnel, roughly 8.2 km in total). Geoje is a major
  shipbuilding hub and a scenic tourist destination; it was also the site of the **POW camp
  during the Korean War** (the Geoje POW Uprising of 1952 brought international attention to
  conditions in United Nations Command camps).
- **Namhae Island** (Namhae-do): Connected to the mainland at Hadong by the Namhae Bridge
  (built 1973, South Korea's first suspension bridge) and to Sacheon and the south coast by
  the 2007 Changseon–Sacheon Bridge. Known for its terraced rice paddies, traditional
  villages (including a "German Village" established in the 1970s by Korean contract workers
  who settled after returning from West Germany), and a developing marine-tourism sector.
- **Bijindo, Somaemuldo, Yokjido** and hundreds of smaller islets along the Hallyeo Maritime
  National Park corridor (국립해상공원), stretching from Tongyeong to Yeosu, designated in
  1968 as one of South Korea's first maritime national parks.

## Major Cities and Urban Centers

**Changwon** (창원시) is the provincial capital and the province's largest city, with a
population of approximately 1.02 million (2024). It was established as a **planned
industrial city** under President Park Chung-hee's Heavy and Chemical Industrialization
(중화학공업화) policy: the Changwon National Industrial Complex was designated in 1974,
and the city proper was formally established in 1980. In **July 2010**, Changwon merged
with the adjacent cities of **Masan** and **Jinhae** to form a single integrated city
(통합시), the largest municipal merger in Korean history; in January 2022 Changwon was
designated a special-case city (*teukryesi*, 특례시), a status granted to cities exceeding
one million residents. Masan had been
a major export-processing zone and port since the 1970s; Jinhae is the headquarters
of the Republic of Korea Navy.

**Jinju** (진주시, population ~340,000) is historically the most important city in the
upper province. It served as the administrative seat of Gyeongsang Province during much
of the Joseon period and is famed for Jinjuseong Fortress and the annual Jinju Lantern
Festival (*Yudeungno Chukje*), held each October on the Nam River in commemoration of
the 1592 battle. Jinju is also home to Gyeongsang National University, the province's
flagship public university.

**Geoje** (거제시, population ~250,000) is the shipbuilding capital of South Korea's
southern coast. **Samsung Heavy Industries** maintains one of its two main yards on Geoje
(established 1977); **Hanwha Ocean** (formerly Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering,
DSME, which was acquired by Hanwha Group in 2023) has its primary shipyard on Geoje's
Okpo Bay. Together, these two yards have built a significant share of the world's LNG
carriers and very large crude carriers (VLCCs) since the 1980s.

**Tongyeong** (통영시, population ~125,000) is a port city on a peninsula jutting into
the Hallyeo Sea, known for its traditional nacreous lacquerware (*자개장*), its connection
to composer Yun Isang (윤이상, 1917–1995), and the annual **Tongyeong International Music
Festival** established in his honour. Tongyeong's Hallyeo Maritime National Park and
seafood markets attract significant domestic tourism.

**Sacheon** (사천시) hosts a significant aerospace and defense manufacturing cluster,
including facilities of Korean Air's aerospace division and the Korea Aerospace Industries
(KAI) final assembly line for the T-50 Golden Eagle trainer and FA-50 light combat aircraft.

## Economy

South Gyeongsang Province is one of South Korea's most industrialized regions. The
economy is anchored by maritime industry, advanced manufacturing, and defense systems:

### Shipbuilding and Maritime Industries

The Geoje yards of Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean together constitute one of
the world's largest concentrations of high-value shipbuilding capacity, producing LNG carriers,
very large crude carriers (VLCCs), FPSOs (floating production storage and offloading vessels),
and container ships. In the peak year 2011, Korean shipyards (with Geoje at the center) received
more than 35% of global new ship orders by gross tonnage.

A prolonged global shipping downturn (2016–2021) caused severe contraction: Hanwha Ocean
(then DSME) required a ₩4.2 trillion government-backed restructuring in 2017. Recovery
came from the resurgence in LNG carrier orders driven by Europe's post-2022 energy
diversification following the Russian invasion of Ukraine; by 2023, Geoje yards were operating
near capacity. The pivot to liquefied ammonia and methanol-fuelled "green ships" is underway
as the industry seeks to comply with the International Maritime Organization's 2030–2050
decarbonisation targets.

### Defence and Aerospace

The **Changwon National Industrial Complex** houses defence contractors producing firearms,
artillery, and military vehicles. Hanwha Defense (a unit of Hanwha Group) produces the K9
Thunder self-propelled howitzer in Changwon, the most widely exported Korean artillery
system (adopted by Finland, India, Norway, Poland, Turkey, and others). **Sacheon**'s
aerospace cluster is the production hub for the KAI T-50 Golden Eagle and the developing
KF-21 Boramae next-generation fighter, a joint Korea–Indonesia programme.

The Republic of Korea Navy's main fleet command is headquartered in **Jinhae** (now part of
Changwon), and Jinhae is the site of the annual Jinhae Gunhang Festival (*Gunhang-je*,
April), Korea's largest cherry-blossom festival, attracting more than 3 million visitors
per year.

### Petrochemicals and Refining

Connecting to [Ulsan](ulsan.md)'s vast Onsan–Ulsan complex, the Gyeongnam coastal strip
hosts satellite petrochemical and speciality-chemical facilities. LG Chem and Lotte
Chemical have plants in the Masan and Geoje industrial complexes, producing polymers and
intermediates for downstream industries throughout the region.

### Automotive Components

Changwon and the surrounding corridor supply automotive components to the Hyundai and Kia
assembly plants in [Ulsan](ulsan.md) and [Gwangju](gwangju.md). The cluster is a key
node in the Korean automotive supply chain, producing transmissions, brake systems, and
precision metal parts.

### Agriculture and Fisheries

The lower Nakdong valley remains productive agricultural land: rice, strawberries (the
Jinju and Sancheong areas), persimmons (Hadong), and green tea (Hadong's *Hwagae* market
is one of Korea's most celebrated traditional tea-trading venues). The south coast hosts
extensive aquaculture operations producing oysters, sea bream, and abalone. Tongyeong
and Geoje are centres of the oyster industry, with Gyeongnam accounting for roughly 70%
of South Korea's oyster production.

## History

### Three Kingdoms and Unified Silla

During the [Three Kingdoms Period](../history/three-kingdoms-period.md), the eastern part of
the province was part of the **Gaya Confederacy** (42–562 CE), a loosely allied polity
of iron-producing chieftains along the Nakdong River. Gaya's iron metallurgy was traded
across the Korean peninsula and to Japan; archaeological finds in the region—elaborate
gold-gilt crowns, bronze mirrors, and iron armour—testify to sophisticated craft
traditions. Gaya was absorbed by the Silla kingdom between the 5th and 6th centuries.
Silla's capital at Gyeongju (now in North Gyeongsang) dominated the eastern Yeongnam
region after 668.

### Joseon and the 1592 Japanese Invasions

During the [Joseon Dynasty](../history/joseon-dynasty.md) (1392–1910), **Jinju** served as
the administrative centre of South Gyeongsang (Gyeongsang Namdo). The province became
a major battleground during the **Imjin War** (임진왜란, 1592–1598), the Japanese invasions
under Toyotomi Hideyoshi. The two **Sieges of Jinju** (1592 and 1593) are landmarks of
Korean resistance: in the First Siege (October 1592), Korean forces under Kim Si-min
successfully repulsed a Japanese force roughly ten times their number; in the Second Siege
(June 1593), a Japanese army of approximately 90,000 overwhelmed the fortress, resulting
in the fall of Jinju and mass civilian deaths at the Nam River.

Admiral Yi Sun-sin (이순신, 1545–1598), Korea's most celebrated naval commander, won
decisive battles in the coastal waters of Gyeongnam: the **Battle of Hansando** (Hansan
Island, August 1592) destroyed more than 70 Japanese warships using the "crane-wing"
(*hakik*) formation and blocked Japanese naval supply lines. The port city of **Tongyeong** takes
its name from the *Tongjeyeong* (統制營), the naval command of the three southern provinces
of which Yi was the first commander (appointed 1593); the headquarters was relocated to the
present-day city in 1604, after Yi's death at the Battle of Noryang in 1598.

### Modern Industrialisation

Following [Japanese colonial rule](../history/japanese-colonial-period.md) (1910–1945),
during which Masan and Busan served as major colonial ports and transport nodes, the
province was a focal point of President Park Chung-hee's 1970s industrialisation drive.
The declaration of **Ulsan** as a Special Industrial District (1962) and the establishment
of the **Changwon Machine Industry Complex** (1974) anchored a coastal industrial belt
that stretched from Busan through Changwon, Masan, and Geoje. The Geoje yards launched
their first vessel in 1981 (DSME/Daewoo) and 1977 (Samsung), initiating the province's
rise to global shipbuilding prominence.

The **Busan Perimeter** (August–September 1950) during the [Korean War](../history/korean-war.md)
was the last defensive line established around Busan and the lower Nakdong River. UN
and ROK forces, under General Walton Walker, held the perimeter against North Korean
forces from August 4 to September 16, 1950—one of the most intense defensive battles in
modern military history—before the Inchon Landing reversed the strategic situation.

### Geoje POW Camp Uprising (1952)

The **Geoje POW Uprising** (May 1952) was a significant incident in the Korean War
armistice negotiations. Approximately 170,000 North Korean and Chinese POWs held at
the UN camp on Geoje Island rioted and briefly held US Brigadier General Francis Dodd
hostage. The incident embarrassed the UN Command and complicated repatriation negotiations
at Panmunjom, highlighting disagreements over voluntary versus forced repatriation—a
central issue in the armistice talks.

## Culture and Identity

South Gyeongsang Province forms the southern half of the **Yeongnam** (영남) cultural
region, which stretches from the Nakdong basin to the Korea Strait. The region has
historically been the heartland of Confucian scholarship (the Joseon-era Yeongnam School
or *Yeongjae hakpa* was centered in Jinju and Andong), and its conservative political
character has persisted into the modern era, though newer industrial cities like Changwon
have more mixed political profiles.

### Jinju Fortress and the 1592 Siege Legacy

Jinjuseong (진주성) is now a national historic site and park. The Jinju National Museum
(진주박물관) houses armour, weapons, and documents from the Imjin War, presenting the siege
and the heroic stories of resistance fighters including *Nongae* (논개, a *gisaeng* courtesan
celebrated for pulling a Japanese general into the Nam River during the 1593 siege). The
annual **Jinju Lantern Festival** (*Yudeung*) releases thousands of traditional lotus
lanterns on the Nam River in October, drawing hundreds of thousands of visitors.

### Jinhae Cherry Blossom Festival

The annual **Jinhae Gunhang Festival** (진해군항제) in early April transforms Jinhae's
naval-base town into a sea of *yoshino* cherry blossoms. With more than 360,000 cherry
trees lining streets and naval base avenues, the festival is one of South Korea's most
attended seasonal events. Admiral Yi Sun-sin is commemorated through memorial ceremonies
during the festival.

### Cuisine

Gyeongnam cuisine is known for its bold, salty, and spiced flavours. **Milmyeon**
(밀면, cold wheat-noodle broth) was invented in Busan during the Korean War by displaced
northern Koreans who substituted wheat flour for buckwheat (unavailable in wartime) and
became a Busan-region staple. **Chamchi-guksu** (참치국수, tuna noodles) and **Haemul
pajeon** (seafood spring-onion pancakes) are regional favourites in the port cities.
Hadong's traditional tea culture is centred on green tea grown on the slopes of Jirisan;
the Hwagae Tea Festival each spring celebrates the first harvest.

## Administrative Division

South Gyeongsang Province is subdivided into **8 cities** (*si*) and **10 counties** (*gun*):

**Cities (Si)**:
- **Changwon** (provincial capital, incorporating former Masan and Jinhae; merged 2010)
- **Jinju**
- **Geoje**
- **Tongyeong**
- **Sacheon**
- **Gimhae** (at the western edge of the Busan metropolitan area)
- **Miryang**
- **Yangsan**

**Counties (Gun)**:
- Uiryeong, Haman, Changnyeong, Goseong, Namhae, Hadong, Sancheong,
  Hamyang, Geochang, Hapcheon

(Note: Gimhae and Yangsan are functionally part of the greater Busan metropolitan
corridor despite remaining within Gyeongnam provincial jurisdiction. Miryang, on the
upper Nakdong, is a historic transit city and administrative centre for the northern part
of the province.)

## Challenges and Future Directions

**Demographic Decline**: The province's population peaked in the late 1990s and has
declined steadily since, driven by youth out-migration to Seoul and the Busan metropolitan
area. Rural counties such as Hadong, Namhae, and Sancheong have median ages above 55,
straining agricultural labour supply and local service provision.

**Post-Shipbuilding Transition**: The Geoje economy experienced severe contraction during
the 2016–2021 shipbuilding downturn; local governments invested in tourism infrastructure
(cable cars, waterfront development) and SME support. The 2023–2025 LNG-carrier order
revival has provided breathing room, but long-term transition to green shipping and
offshore wind vessel construction is a strategic priority.

**Defence Industry Growth**: The K9 howitzer's international export success and KF-21
programme have made defence manufacturing a growth pillar in Changwon and Sacheon,
partially offsetting the volatility of commercial shipbuilding cycles.

**Environmental Restoration**: The Nakdong River ecosystem faces cumulative pressure
from agricultural runoff, industrial effluents, and the green-algae blooms (녹조, *nokjo*,
documented annually since the 2010s) that impair water quality downstream.
The Four Rivers Restoration Project (2009–2012), which installed barrages along the
Nakdong, has been controversial: critics argue the barrages intensified algal blooms
by reducing water flow velocity.

**Tourism Diversification**: The Hallyeo Maritime National Park, Namhae Island's terraced
village landscape, Tongyeong's arts heritage, and Geoje's historical POW camp museum are
being developed into distinctive tourism products to reduce reliance on industrial employment.
